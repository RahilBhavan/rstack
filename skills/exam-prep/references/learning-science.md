# Learning science → design rules

Dunlosky et al. (2013, *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*) reviewed ten common
study techniques. **Practice testing** and **distributed practice** got the highest utility
rating. **Summarization, highlighting, and rereading** got low ratings. Interleaving
(mixing problem types) had a large effect in a randomized trial (Rohrer et al. 2020,
d = 0.83), partly because it forces the student to choose a method, not just execute one.
Alternating worked examples with problems to solve is also well supported.

A wall of "ultra detailed notes" is mostly rereading material. Build these instead:

## Study guide structure (per topic)

1. **Try first** (2-4 questions, answers hidden). Attempting before reading improves what
   sticks from the explanation that follows, even when the attempt fails.
2. **Core idea**: the explanation, from first principles, as short as it can be while
   complete. One diagram or graph where the concept is visual (supply/demand shifts,
   regression lines, circuits).
3. **Worked example**: one fully solved problem in the exam's format.
4. **Your turn**: a similar problem with the answer hidden.
5. **Traps**: the 1-3 most common wrong answers and why they're wrong.
6. **Key terms** in the marking scheme's exact wording.

## Across the guide
- **Mixed review section** at the end: 10-20 questions drawn from all topics in shuffled
  order, so the student has to recognize which concept applies.
- **Review plan**: if the exam date is known, a short schedule spreading review across
  the remaining days (e.g. day 1 topics A-C, day 3 mixed set, day 5 full practice exam).
  Short, spaced sessions beat one long one.
- **One-page summary** at the end only if asked or if the exam allows a cheat sheet.

## When the user asks for detailed notes
Give them the full detail they asked for; don't trim it to a summary. Put that detail inside
the structure above (try-first questions, core idea, worked example, traps), so the
detailed version still makes them retrieve, not just reread.

## What not to do
- Don't produce long prose summaries of every slide. Compress; the slides already exist.
- Don't put answers next to questions where the eye catches them. Hide them (see output-format.md).
